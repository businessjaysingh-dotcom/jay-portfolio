What I'm working on now

I'm about a month into learning how AI search decides what to recommend. I'm building the tooling as I go rather than only reading about it.

What I understand so far: a model learns patterns during training. When search is enabled, an answer can also draw on live pages retrieved for the question. Not every answer uses live search. That distinction matters when I compare how different engines describe the same company.

I start by checking whether the relevant crawlers can read a site. I look at robots.txt, but I also check the page content and access restrictions. Training and search need separate checks: GPTBot and ClaudeBot serve training, while OAI-SearchBot and Claude-SearchBot serve search. Google-Extended is a separate control, not the switch for inclusion in Google Search. I can inspect those rules at a site's /robots.txt address.

The part that surprised me is that a question can lead to several searches. Google describes a query fan-out approach for its AI search features. I’m learning to look at the spread of related questions rather than assume there is one fixed position to win.

Traditional SEO still matters. Making useful pages that search engines can crawl and understand remains part of the work.

I don't have client results in this yet. What I have is the tooling, a real audit of ten sites, and a month of notes. That's the honest state of it.

I check the source documentation: OpenAI crawlers, Anthropic crawlers, and Google AI search.
