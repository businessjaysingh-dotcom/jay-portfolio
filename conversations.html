Anatomy · four gates

The framework

A lead only moves forward when it clears a gate. The gates are code, not vibes — which is why the system never asks for the meeting too early, and never asks a question it already has the answer to.

01

Rapport

exit gate: 8 exchanges OR score ≥ 40

Zero qualifying questions allowed. The system answers their questions before asking its own, and volunteers a real number about itself first. Reciprocity before extraction.

02

Qualifying

exit gate: 3 answers + score ≥ 50 OR strong signal + score ≥ 40

Seven questions, one at a time, never batched. A refused question gets one rephrase at a lower bar, then it's abandoned permanently — asking a third time is how accounts get blocked.

03

Educating

rule: give the fix away for real

The playbook is not held hostage. The system hands over the actual mechanism, with numbers. Someone who got real help asks to work together — you don't have to push.

04

Booking  /  walking away

rule: one offer only · never a second ask · never a price

The link drops once. If they don't take it, the system keeps helping and shuts up about the meeting. And if the lead can't afford it or isn't the right person, it says so kindly and closes the thread — see conversation three.

Where this system is weak, stated plainly: a 38.5% cold reply rate is strong, and the conversation engine holds its own for eleven days against a real operator. But only four threads have reached a sent meeting link. The top of the funnel works; the conversion to meetings does not work yet.

I know exactly why: the qualification filter passes 1.7% of scraped leads, and the score gate for booking sits at 60 while the average logged score is 19. The system is correctly refusing to pitch people it hasn't qualified — I built the brakes before I built the engine. Loosening that gate without fixing lead quality would just produce worse conversations faster.

Which is the whole reason I'm showing you this instead of a résumé. I can build the machine that starts 99 conversations for pennies apiece. Put me on the live end of one and the funnel is finished.

About me

How I actually work

I build the thing rather than describe it. Everything above — the scraper, the qualifier, the conversation engine, the scoring model, the CRM, the crash supervisor, the phone control layer — is one system I designed and wrote alone, and it has been running unattended in production since June 2026.

What I care about is the reasoning underneath a conversation. Anyone can write a message sequence. The interesting problem is encoding the judgement: at what point has this person told you enough that a question becomes intrusive? When does giving away the answer for free make you more credible instead of less? When is the correct move to tell someone not to buy? I turned each of those into a gate with a condition, and then I let the logs tell me which gates were wrong.

I'm also happy to be the one holding the phone. The system is deliberately built to hand off — it qualifies, it earns trust, it offers the meeting, and then a human takes it from there. I built the hand-off because I want to be on the other side of it.
